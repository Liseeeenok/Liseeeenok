RIS

Frontend October CMS Node.js 4 years in the project

A research information system for IDSTU SB RAS. I joined when it was already running and spent four years growing it: new sections, reworked interfaces, a researcher profile and automatic document generation.

The task

The institute tracked its output by hand: publications, patents and projects lived in spreadsheets and in people's heads. Supporting documents — author certificates, extracts, the annual report — were assembled in Word over weeks, and wrong names were hunted by eye. Every report started from zero even though the data was already in the system.

My role

Frontend developer: section interfaces, the researcher profile, search and analytics. I also collected requirements — institute staff came with requests, I turned them into tasks and agreed priorities with the manager.

What I built
  • Reworked the main sections: publication list, search with filters, institute structure, projects.
  • Built the researcher page: papers, projects, awards and yearly figures in one place.
  • Added publication analytics: types, indexing (WoS, Scopus, RSCI), year-over-year dynamics.
  • Connected automatic generation of author certificates and other supporting documents.
  • Removed manual assembly of the annual report: figures come from the system, not from exports.
  • Iterated on the interface using staff feedback — from wording to column order in tables.
How it works

The core is October CMS on Laravel: domain logic lives in plugins, the theme renders on top. Documents are produced by a separate Node.js service that takes data for a person or department, fills a template and returns the finished file.

Outcome

Preparing supporting documents became about three times faster, assembling the annual report five times. It was verified by real work: staff prepared their reports in the system and the screens were fixed from their feedback.

Constraints

A legacy project: it was launched by a developer who later left, on an old October CMS version. Some decisions had to fit the existing database schema rather than replacing it.

What I would improve

I would redesign the researcher profile — too much data on one screen — and speed up report generation for past years, which is the heaviest operation in the system.
